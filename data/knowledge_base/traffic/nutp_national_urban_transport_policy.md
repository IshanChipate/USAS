# National Urban Transport Policy (NUTP) — Sustainable Urban Mobility and Congestion Management

**Author:** Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA), Government of India  
**Publication Year:** 2014  
**Scope:** Urban Mobility, Traffic Demand Management, and Multimodal Public Transport for Indian Cities  
**Official Reference:** National Urban Transport Policy Framework 2014  

## 1. Core Paradigm: Moving People Rather Than Vehicles
The National Urban Transport Policy establishes that urban transportation planning must prioritize people over private vehicular throughput. Investments must shift from highway widening and grade separators towards integrated public transit systems, pedestrian safety, and non-motorized transport (NMT) infrastructure.

## 2. Public Transit and Corridor Efficiency
To manage vehicular pressure in rapidly growing Maharashtra cities:
- Development of organized public bus services with dedicated rights-of-way (Bus Rapid Transit or queue-jump lanes at major intersections).
- Transition to clean fuel and electric buses with intelligent scheduling based on real-time automated passenger counters (APC).
- Fare and timetable integration across suburban rail, metro corridors, municipal buses, and feeder auto-rickshaw/shuttle routes.

## 3. Traffic Demand Management (TDM) and Congestion Control
Where street capacity is physically constrained:
- On-street parking regulation: Replace free on-street parking with dynamic, demand-based parking tariffs to discourage private vehicle commuting to central commercial districts.
- Enforce strict no-parking corridors along major transit trunk routes.
- Staggered working hours for major public and private institutional sectors to flatten morning and evening peak congestion spikes.
- Signal optimization using Adaptive Traffic Control Systems (ATCS) that adjust signal green cycles dynamically according to sensor/CCTV-measured queue lengths.

## 4. Pedestrian and Non-Motorized Transport (NMT) Protection
- Mandatory continuous, unobstructed footpaths conforming to Indian Roads Congress (IRC:103-2012) standards (minimum 1.8m width for residential streets; 2.5m+ for commercial zones).
- Grade-level pedestrian zebra crossings with refuge islands on multi-lane carriageways spaced no more than 150m apart.
- Protected two-way cycle tracks connecting transit stations with educational institutions and dense residential zones.

## 5. Assessment and Indicator Guidance
Traffic sustainability must be interpreted accurately:
- Direct congestion indicators (average travel time delay, TomTom congestion index %) directly measure flow impedance.
- Registered motor vehicle counts represent a "traffic-pressure proxy" reflecting vehicle ownership growth rather than real-time arterial speed or delay. Planners must not treat registration counts as congestion measurements without local traffic-flow surveys.
