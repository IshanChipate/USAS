# Maharashtra Municipal Solid Waste Management Action Plan and Processing Guidelines

**Author:** Maharashtra Pollution Control Board (MPCB), Government of Maharashtra  
**Publication Year:** 2023  
**Scope:** Compliance with Solid Waste Management Rules 2016 for Maharashtra Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)  
**Official Reference:** MPCB Annual MSW Report & Implementation Framework 2023  

## 1. Statutory Mandate and Treatment Benchmarks
Under Rule 15 of the Solid Waste Management (SWM) Rules 2016, all municipal corporations and councils in Maharashtra must achieve 100% door-to-door waste collection and segregation at source into biodegradable (wet), non-biodegradable (dry), and domestic hazardous fractions. The targeted waste treatment efficiency benchmark is minimum 85% of total generated waste treated through scientific processing, preventing unprocessed waste from reaching open dump sites.

## 2. Biodegradable (Wet Waste) Processing
Wet organic waste constitutes between 45% and 55% of municipal waste in Maharashtra cities:
- Decentralized bio-methanation (CBG / biogas plants) and aerobic composting units for large bulk waste generators (hotels, commercial complexes, housing societies exceeding 5,000 sq.m).
- Centralized composting facilities with leachate treatment systems for municipal collection streams.
- City composting standards conforming to Fertilizer Control Order (FCO) parameters for heavy metal concentrations and organic carbon content.

## 3. Dry Waste Segregation, Material Recovery, and Refuse-Derived Fuel (RDF)
Dry recyclable and combustible waste management requires:
- Material Recovery Facilities (MRF) operated in partnership with informal waste-picker collectives and authorized recycling enterprises.
- Compulsory segregation of non-recyclable high-calorific fraction (plastics, textiles, multilayer packaging) into Refuse-Derived Fuel (RDF).
- Long-term off-take agreements between municipal corporations and nearby cement manufacturing kilns and thermal power plants for RDF co-processing as per CPCB guidelines.

## 4. Legacy Waste Dumpsite Remediation (Biomining)
Open dump sites pose severe groundwater contamination (leachate migration) and methane fire risks:
- Complete biomining and bio-remediation of legacy dumpsites across all Class-I and Class-II urban centers in Maharashtra.
- Reclamation of prime urban land for designated green belts and public parks after comprehensive soil safety testing.

## 5. Monitoring Indicators and Urban Governance
Urban sustainability assessments must report:
- Total waste generated versus total treated in Metric Tonnes per Day (MT/day).
- Percentage of door-to-door segregation verified by spot-checks.
- Operational treatment efficiency ratio: (Waste Treated / Waste Generated) * 100.
- Elimination of unlined dumping and establishment of sanitary landfilling solely for non-reactive processing residues (< 15% of total generation).
