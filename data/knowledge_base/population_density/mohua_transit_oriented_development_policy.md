# National Transit Oriented Development (TOD) Policy and Urban Density Carrying Capacity Guidelines

**Author:** Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA), Government of India  
**Publication Year:** 2017  
**Scope:** High-Density Compact Urban Growth, Ward-Level Infrastructure Carrying Capacity, and Mixed-Use Development  
**Official Reference:** National TOD Policy Framework MoHUA-2017  

## 1. Principles of Compact, Sustainable Urban Density
High urban population density is a characteristic of Indian cities that can either strain municipal services or enable highly efficient, low-carbon urbanism. Compact development concentrates population and commercial activities within walkable distance (500m to 800m) of rapid transit stations, minimizing sprawl and private vehicular trip lengths.

## 2. Infrastructure Carrying Capacity Assessment
Higher population density must not outpace municipal basic service thresholds:
- **Water Supply:** Minimum per capita supply benchmark of 135 liters per capita per day (lpcd) for municipal corporation areas with underground sewerage.
- **Sewerage & Drainage:** 100% underground sewerage network coverage with centralized sewage treatment plants (STP) treating effluent to CPCB discharge standards (BOD < 10 mg/L).
- **Solid Waste:** Segregated collection infrastructure scaled to ward population (average 450 to 500 grams per capita per day generation rate).
- **Public Open Space:** Strict preservation of open space reservations in dense wards to prevent the total loss of permeable soil surfaces and urban heat sink capability.

## 3. Floor Space Index (FSI/FAR) and Mixed-Use Zoning
- Incentive FSI/FAR in designated TOD Influence Zones along mass transit corridors to facilitate compact vertical redevelopment.
- Mandatory inclusion of 15% to 20% floor space for Economically Weaker Section (EWS) and Low-Income Group (LIG) affordable housing within high-density redevelopment clusters.
- Mixed-use street fronts (active commercial retail on ground floors, offices and residential units above) to maintain 24/7 street vitality and natural surveillance.

## 4. Ward-Level Pressure Mitigation Strategies
In wards where population density exceeds local carrying capacity:
- Prioritize decentralized civic amenities (primary health centers, Anganwadis, municipal schools) within 400m walking radius.
- Implement stormwater retention basins and mandatory rainwater harvesting (RWH) recharge pits in all plots exceeding 300 sq.m to mitigate urban flooding in heavily built-up areas.
- Create multi-modal pedestrian feeder links connecting high-density residential wards directly to high-capacity transit lines.

## 5. Analytical Interpretation in Decision Support
In the USAS framework:
- Population density (Census 2011 persons per km²) is evaluated as an urban pressure indicator, where higher density reflects greater infrastructure loading and congestion potential.
- Density scores must be interpreted alongside ward-level service availability (water, sewerage, waste treatment) rather than as an absolute negative attribute, recognizing that well-serviced compact density is environmentally preferable to low-density sprawling expansion.
