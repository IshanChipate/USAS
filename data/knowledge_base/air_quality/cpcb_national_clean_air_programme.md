# National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) — Strategy and Implementation Guidelines

**Author:** Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB), Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Government of India  
**Publication Year:** 2019 (Updated 2024)  
**Scope:** Urban Air Quality Management for Non-Attainment Cities in Maharashtra  
**Official Reference:** CPCB NCAP Guidelines No. B-33014/7/2019-AQM  

## 1. Executive Summary and Objectives
The National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) is a long-term, time-bound, national-level strategy to tackle the air pollution problem across the country in a comprehensive manner. The objective is to achieve a 20% to 30% reduction in particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10) concentrations by 2024–2026 across designated non-attainment cities, including key urban centers in Maharashtra such as Pune, Mumbai, Nagpur, Nashik, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, and Solapur.

## 2. City-Specific Air Action Plans
Each urban local body (ULB) must establish an Air Quality Management Cell responsible for:
- Implementing continuous ambient air quality monitoring stations (CAAQMS) reporting hourly PM2.5 and PM10 values.
- Identifying localized pollution hotspots along high-density traffic corridors, construction clusters, and industrial peripheries.
- Establishing micro-action plans addressing seasonal peaks, particularly during winter temperature inversion periods.

## 3. Road Dust and Construction Dust Abatement
Road dust resuspension contributes between 25% and 40% of ambient PM10 in Maharashtra cities:
- Mandate mechanized sweeping and high-pressure water washing of paved arterial and sub-arterial roads.
- Enforce green buffering and end-to-end paving or grassing of road shoulders to prevent unpaved soil exposure.
- Enforce Construction and Demolition (C&D) Waste Management Rules (2016) with mandatory wind-breaking barricades (minimum 6m height) around active construction sites and wet-suppression mist guns.

## 4. Vehicular Emission Interventions
Vehicular transport is the primary urban contributor to roadside PM2.5 and NO2:
- Accelerated phase-out of commercial diesel vehicles older than 15 years and transition of public bus fleets to CNG/Electric (EV).
- Enforcement of strict automated Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate validation linked to Vahan registration databases.
- Creation of low-emission corridors and non-motorized transport (NMT) priority zones around congested central business districts.

## 5. Monitoring, Verification, and Performance Metrics
Intervention efficacy must be tracked using standardized indicators:
- Annual average PM2.5 and PM10 levels relative to National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS: 40 µg/m³ for PM2.5 and 60 µg/m³ for PM10).
- Number of days in Satisfactory/Moderate AQI categories versus Poor, Very Poor, and Severe categories.
- Implementation rate of green vegetative screens (multi-tier plantations) between major highways and adjacent residential settlements.
